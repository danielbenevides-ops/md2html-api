# My MRR Goal Isn't $10K — It's $1

I just launched [MD2HTML API](http://147.15.103.217/md2html/) — a 10-endpoint Markdown-to-HTML conversion API with crypto payments, rate limiting, and API-key auth. I built the entire thing with AI as my co-pilot: FastAPI, Postgres, Redis, and Coinbase Commerce for X402-style pay-per-call billing.

Here's the thing though — my goal isn't to hit $10K MRR. It's to hit **$1**.

Not $1,000. Not even $100. One single dollar of recurring revenue.

Why? Because $1 MRR is the hardest dollar you'll ever earn. It's the dollar that proves a stranger found your product, understood it, pulled out a wallet, and *paid you* on purpose. It's the dollar that tells you the thing you built has a market. After that first dollar, growth is a mechanics problem. Before it, you're just guessing in a vacuum.

Most indie hackers I see on here are optimizing for scale they haven't earned yet — funnels, email sequences, pricing pages with "Enterprise — Contact Us." I'm doing the opposite. The entire product is live right now with a free tier (100 calls/hour) and a paid tier at **$0.50/month**. Crypto only — BTC, ETH, USDC. No Stripe. No KYC. Anyone in any country can pay.

I picked crypto on purpose. It removes the friction that kills international signups. Developer in Lagos wants to pay? They can. Someone in Buenos Aires? Same.

I'll be posting weekly updates here. The next big milestones, in order:

1. **$1 MRR** — first paid user
2. **$10 MRR** — first ten users
3. **$100 MRR** — product-market fit whisper
4. **$1,000 MRR** — quit-a-day-job conversation (not yet!)

I'm also writing a 5-part Dev.to series on the build — covering the idea, the stack, crypto payments, security hardening, and distribution. All in public, real numbers, real code.

If you've been lurking on a side project — ship it. Set the goal at $1. The first dollar is the only one that matters.
